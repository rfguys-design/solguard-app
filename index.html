A year of glare. One file.

Ranked. Mapped. Ready to plan with.

Every ~1-mile stretch, both directions. Ranked by glare VMT: miles driven into a low sun, weighted by how blinding. Traffic, hours by month and time of day, fatal crashes. All in.
How it works
Glare VMT = vehicles passing while the stretch is in glare × its length: the unit agencies already count in, fair to short and long stretches, and it adds up by county or route. The ranking weights it by severity (sun dead ahead in the middle of the glare band = 1). Hotspot = top 5% of all California stretches. Ranks are statewide, also in a county file.
Model: the Standard setting (sun within 12° of straight ahead, 0–18° up, the one checked against crash records), not the sliders; hills included; clear sky (cloud only lowers these numbers); every day, every 5 minutes through the morning and evening low-sun windows. Traffic: Caltrans counts × a typical hourly pattern. Crashes: NHTSA FARS fatal crashes 2015–2024, glare as coded by the investigator, and those that happened while this model says the stretch was in glare. Times are Pacific local time. The sun's yearly path repeats almost exactly, so 2027 serves any year.
